Are We Delivering Where Need Is Greatest?

This tab answers a critical question: are the people most severely affected by Hurricane Melissa the same people FFTP Jamaica's programmes are reaching? To answer it accurately, this dashboard uses two complementary metrics. The primary metric — People Reached — converts each type of intervention into an estimated number of people benefiting, using internationally-standardised conversion factors (SPHERE 2018, FFTP Jamaica household standard, MOE Jamaica). A water system reaching 300 people counts for more than a roof repair reaching 3 people (Jamaica average household size). The secondary metric — Units Delivered — shows the raw count of interventions for operational transparency. Use the toggle to switch between views. The NVI-Ideal column shows what each parish's share of total people reached should be, based on its vulnerability score. Red gaps are under-served parishes — priority targets for new unrestricted donations.

What the Data Shows — Four Things to Understand About Current Delivery

🔴 ST. ELIZABETH IS RECEIVING FAR MORE THAN ITS VULNERABILITY SCORE WARRANTS

St. Elizabeth has received 360 of FFTP's 969 confirmed-parish units — that is 37.2% of everything delivered so far. But its NVI vulnerability score of 8.70 means it should ideally receive around 11.2% of delivery. This concentration is driven by 202 livelihood programme participants and 121 housing repairs, most of which came through Year One donor designations. St. Elizabeth is an important parish — but new funding should be redirected toward parishes with greater unmet need.

🟡 TRELAWNY LOOKS SERVED ON PAPER — BUT ONE PROJECT TELLS THE WHOLE STORY

When you count projects, Trelawny looks under-served. When you count the actual number of units delivered, it appears over-allocated — but that is almost entirely due to a single 184-unit housing repair project. Outside of housing, Trelawny has only 2 water systems and 2 school buildings confirmed. It is a Critical-tier parish (NVI 8.18) whose apparent coverage masks major sectoral gaps. Roof repairs alone do not constitute a full recovery response.

⚠️ HANOVER APPEARS ALIGNED — BUT ONLY BECAUSE OF HOUSING. WASH AND FOOD ARE NEAR-ZERO.

Hanover is Jamaica's second most vulnerable parish, with an NVI score of 9.00 — meaning it suffered near-maximum damage across disaster exposure, water infrastructure, and food security. On the surface, its total unit count looks close to what the NVI would suggest. But almost all of that is housing repairs. Hanover has zero confirmed water systems and only 1 confirmed monthly food kit distributed. For a parish whose water infrastructure was critically compromised by Hurricane Melissa, this is one of the most urgent gaps in the entire portfolio. New funding directed to Hanover should prioritise WASH and livelihoods, not housing.

⚠️ MORE THAN 6,000 UNITS HAVE NO CONFIRMED PARISH — THIS ANALYSIS ONLY SHOWS PART OF THE PICTURE

805 housing repairs (60% of FFTP's housing target) and 5,249 food security kits — each representing one family of three fed for one month — have not yet been assigned to a confirmed parish. Until they are, this tab can only show alignment for the 969 units where a parish is known. This is the single most important data task in the portfolio — assigning these units to parishes will change the alignment picture significantly and unlock the final new investment allocation recommendations. This is being actively resolved by the programme team.

Are We Delivering the Right Types of Support — and Enough of It?

Each of FFTP Jamaica's five sectors — Housing, Water & Sanitation (WASH), Food Security, Livelihoods, and Infrastructure — has its own vulnerability score from the NVI and its own delivery completion rate. This tab asks whether the sectors with the greatest need are the sectors receiving the most attention. The Gap Score shown on each card combines both signals: a high NVI score plus a low completion rate produces a high Gap Score — meaning that sector needs urgent new investment. Note: completion rates here are calculated from confirmed parish-level delivery only. Food security and housing carry significant unassigned volumes (see Tab 5), so their completion rates appear lower than the full-portfolio picture.

How Donor Funding Is Structured — and Where the Gaps Are

FFTP Jamaica has raised $9.03 million for Hurricane Melissa recovery — a substantial achievement. But not all funding is equal in flexibility: 57.4% is restricted to specific projects or geographies chosen by the donor, and 42.6% is unrestricted, allowing FFTP to direct it where the NVI says need is greatest. This tab shows how donor designations have shaped the portfolio, where the alignment between donor intent and community vulnerability is strong, and where gaps remain that new, flexible funding can close.

Year One delivery was shaped by donor designations — when a donor specifies a parish, programming follows that designation regardless of NVI ranking. This is appropriate for restricted funding. The data below shows where those designations have left gaps against the highest-need populations. Each row is an investment opportunity for new, unrestricted donations.

Earmarking Case Study — CIBC Church-School Infrastructure Programme

CIBC has committed funding for 10 school renovation projects, all of which are approved, funded, and waiting to begin. Infrastructure is the sector with the second-highest need gap in the portfolio — 126 non-government-assisted schools were excluded from the Jamaican Government's reconstruction programme, leaving FFTP as one of the only organisations repairing them. The CIBC partnership is a strong example of sector-aligned giving: the donor's focus matches a genuine and documented gap. The main constraint is geographic flexibility — as a fully earmarked sector, FFTP cannot redirect infrastructure funds to higher-NVI parishes if they fall outside CIBC's designated communities.

Where Should New Donations Go? — The NVI-Guided Allocation Plan

This tab shows FFTP Jamaica's recommended allocation of 3,001 project units that have not yet been assigned to a specific parish. These represent the largest single new investment decision in the portfolio — particularly 2,749 monthly food kits and a significant number of housing units. The allocation below is guided by the NVI new investment model: 60–65% NVI parish vulnerability score, 25–30% sector unmet need density, and a pass/fail partner feasibility gate. Infrastructure is excluded — no unassigned infrastructure units exist; see the investment priority table in the NVI Assessment for school-specific recommendations.

⚠️ Housing TBD Alert

The number of unassigned housing units (roof repairs) needs verification — two different figures have appeared in the data extractions. Before this allocation is finalised, the programme team should confirm the exact number. If the higher figure is correct, Westmoreland's recommended housing allocation would increase substantially, given its NVI score of 9.48 and an estimated 134,509 people in need.

⚠️ Food Security Uncertainty

2,749 of 2,776 monthly food kits — each providing one month of nutritional support to a family of 3 — do not yet have a confirmed delivery parish. This means the food security allocation shown above is a recommended target, not a confirmed delivery plan. Assigning these kits to parishes is the single highest-priority data task in the portfolio. Until it is done, the food security coverage rates shown in Tab 1 significantly understate FFTP's actual reach.

⚠️ Operational Capacity Caveat

The partner feasibility gate is applied qualitatively based on current programme team knowledge of church partner networks and community access. Manchester and St. Mary are flagged as requiring Year Two partner assessment before delivery begins — they are included in the recommended allocation on the assumption that assessment will confirm feasibility. The programme team should validate both parishes before committing funds.
